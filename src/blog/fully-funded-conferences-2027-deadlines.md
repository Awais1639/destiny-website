Five **fully funded conferences 2027** calls are open right now, and the first one closes in four days: EGU's travel support needs your abstract by **1 December 2026, 13:00 CET**. After that come the FEBS Young Scientists' Forum (9 December), the IAF Emerging Space Leaders Grant (31 January 2027), the St. Gallen Symposium's Global Ideas Competition (1 February 2027) and the Heidelberg Laureate Forum (11 February 2027). We checked every date and benefit below on the organisers' own pages today.

"Fully funded" means different things at each event, so read the table and the notes carefully. Some cover flights, hotel and registration; others waive fees and add a capped travel contribution.

All five are run by established scientific or academic bodies and charge no fee to apply. You can compare them with other events in our [funded conferences directory](/conferences/).

## EGU 2027 travel support: abstract due 1 December

The European Geosciences Union's Roland Schlich travel support is the most urgent call. To be considered, you must submit your abstract to EGU27 by 1 December 2026, 13:00 CET — earlier than the normal abstract deadline — and request support while submitting. You must be the contact, first and presenting author, and the official page says only 2027 EGU members can submit for support.

- **Early Career Scientist's Travel Support:** for students, PhD candidates and scientists whose highest degree is from the past seven years (status counted on 1 January 2027).
- **Established Scientist's Travel Support:** for established scientists from low, lower-middle and upper-middle income countries.
- **Benefit:** registration fee waiver, refund of the abstract processing charge and, at the committee's discretion, up to €400 towards travel. Decisions come by 9 January 2027.

If you miss 1 December, EGU's separate EDI Participation Support closes on 5 February 2027, 23:59 CET, with a cap of €650 per person.

## FEBS Young Scientists' Forum: closes 9 December

The FEBS Young Scientists' Forum (YSF) is a meeting for PhD students and postdocs held in Pärnu, Estonia, just before the 51st FEBS Congress in Riga, Latvia. Applications close on 9 December 2026. You apply through the Congress abstract system by choosing "YSF Applicant", with an abstract on which you are first author.

FEBS says it will financially support successful applicants from FEBS countries through YSF grants, and you must belong to a FEBS Constituent Society. For students from Pakistan and other non-FEBS countries, the realistic route is the **two places reserved for non-FEBS countries**, funded by IUBMB. Competition for two places is intense, so apply only if your research is a strong fit.

## IAF Emerging Space Leaders Grant: 31 January

The International Astronautical Federation's call for 2027 is open. Its handbook gives the deadline as 31 January 2027, 23:59 Paris time, for students at a recognised university or young professionals in the space sector who are 21 to 35 years old on 1 January 2027.

This is one of the most complete packages on the list. The IAF Secretariat books and pays flights and accommodation in Poznań for 22 September to 2 October 2027, pays registration for the International Astronautical Congress and related workshops, and gives a per-diem. Visas, travel insurance and vaccinations are your own cost.

> You must present at least one paper at IAC 2027 (or join the Next Generation Plenary as a panellist). The IAC abstract deadline is 28 February 2027, so plan your abstract now, alongside your grant application.

You also need an endorsement, two reference letters, short essays and a video of up to one minute. See our [IAF Emerging Space Leaders listing](/conferences/iaf-emerging-space-leaders-iac-2027/) for the summary.

## St. Gallen Symposium: Global Ideas Competition by 1 February

The St. Gallen Symposium in Switzerland (27–29 April 2027) selects its "Leaders of Tomorrow" partly through the Global Ideas Competition. Submit by 1 February 2027, 11:59 p.m. in the last time zone (UTC-12). You must be enrolled in a master's or higher programme on 1 February 2027 and be born in 1997 or later. Bachelor's students are generally not eligible.

You write a short anonymous solution brief and record a video explainer. Selected participants have travel, board and lodging covered, and the three award winners share CHF 20,000. Results arrive by mid-March 2027. Declare any AI tools you use — the organisers treat undeclared use as grounds for disqualification.

## Heidelberg Laureate Forum 2027: apply by 11 February

The Heidelberg Laureate Forum brings young researchers together with laureates in mathematics and computer science. Applications are open until 11 February 2027, 11:59 p.m. Berlin time, for undergraduates and master's students who do not yet hold a master's, PhD students, and postdocs within five years of their PhD (one extra year per child). The forum invites the top 100 applicants in each discipline.

The foundation pays hotel accommodation with breakfast from 11 to 18 September 2027 and meals at the venue. Travel is not covered automatically: accepted participants who cannot get funding from their own university or national funder can apply for an HLFF travel grant from 3 May to 23 June 2027, with a rejection letter as proof. Details are on our [Heidelberg Laureate Forum listing](/conferences/heidelberg-laureate-forum-2027/).

## What to do now

1. **This weekend:** if you are a geoscientist, finish your EGU27 abstract and tick the travel-support request before 1 December, 13:00 CET (17:00 in Pakistan).
2. **By 5 December:** decide whether you fit FEBS YSF; if so, confirm with the FEBS call how non-FEBS applicants are assessed.
3. **In December:** ask two referees for letters for the IAF grant — use our guide on [how to request recommendation letters](/guides/how-to-request-recommendation-letters/).
4. **In January:** draft your St. Gallen solution brief and your Heidelberg motivational statement; give yourself two weeks for each.
5. **Always:** check that your passport is valid well beyond the event dates, because getting a visa appointment can take time.

## Our take for students from Pakistan

These five calls are legitimate and free to apply for, which is the first test any conference should pass. Before you apply to anything else, read our guide on [how to spot fake conferences](/guides/how-to-spot-fake-conferences/): an event that only wants a "registration fee" and promises a certificate is not an opportunity.

Pick the one or two calls that match your field and level rather than applying to all five. Heidelberg suits maths and computer science students; the IAF grant suits engineering and space-science students with a paper in progress; St. Gallen rewards strong writing on a public problem. Selection is competitive and nobody is assured a place, but a well-prepared application also builds your CV for later PhD and scholarship applications.

Remember the visa step. All five events take place in Schengen countries, so a Schengen visa is part of your timeline. Organisers usually send invitation letters only after selection, so keep your bank statements, enrolment letter and passport ready early.
