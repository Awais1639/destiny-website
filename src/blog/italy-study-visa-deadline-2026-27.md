The **Italy study visa deadline** for bachelor's and master's students starting in 2026-27 is **30 November 2026** — four days from today. For the 2027-28 intake, the Italian Ministry of University and Research (MUR) has already fixed the latest date as **31 October 2027**. Both dates come from the Ministry's official procedures for international students, which cover the two academic years together.

If you are still waiting to lodge your visa application for this academic year, treat this week as your last chance. If you are planning for September 2027, this post explains the full sequence and the money you need to show, so you can start early instead of rushing next autumn.

## Who the Italy study visa deadline applies to

The 30 November and 31 October dates apply to students coming for a **bachelor's or master's degree** (laurea and laurea magistrale) who live abroad and pre-enrol through the official [Universitaly portal](https://www.universitaly.it/). Pre-enrolment there is compulsory for these students before they can enrol at an Italian university.

Other kinds of study — PhDs, university "master" courses, Erasmus exchanges, single courses and foundation courses — do not have this fixed national cut-off. For those, the procedures say the visa application must still be made no later than the start of teaching.

> The national date is the latest possible date, not your date. The Ministry allows each university to set earlier visa deadlines for individual programmes, and many do. Always check the programme page or your admission letter first.

## How much money you need to show

The Ministry sets the means of subsistence at **€10,179.85 per year**. The embassy or consulate looks at three things:

- **Where the money comes from.** It must be lawful and traceable. Your own funds are preferred; otherwise, money from your household (up to the fourth degree of kinship) is accepted.
- **Whether it makes sense.** Missions can review your account activity and check that the balance fits your family's income and assets. A large deposit made just before the appointment, with no clear origin, invites questions.
- **Scholarships.** Funding from Italian universities, local governments, foreign institutions and other recognised bodies can count. However, simply applying for a regional grant, or meeting its eligibility rules, is not enough on its own — you need an actual scholarship certificate.

The €10,179.85 is for living costs. Tuition fees, travel and accommodation are separate costs you should plan for. Our [visa money calculator](/tools/visa-money/) shows the Italian figure next to other European countries.

## Accommodation, insurance and language

Besides money, the Ministry's procedures list three more requirements that matter at the embassy and after arrival:

- **Adequate accommodation in Italy.** An application for a place in a university residence, or being eligible for one, does not count as proof of accommodation. Arrange a rental contract, a confirmed residence place or a written hosting arrangement.
- **Health insurance for medical care and hospitalisation.** You must prove cover when you apply for your residence permit. The procedures accept a consular declaration under an agreement between Italy and your country, a foreign policy valid in Italy with no limits on urgent hospitalisation rates, or an Italian policy with a similar declaration from the insurer.
- **Language.** For degree programmes, universities check that you have at least a B2 level — in Italian for Italian-taught programmes, or in the teaching language (usually English) for others.

The visa itself, when issued, is a type-D national visa that the procedures describe as valid for 365 days with multiple entries.

## After you arrive: residence permit and enrolment

Getting the visa is not the end. Within **eight working days** of arriving in Italy, you must apply for a residence permit for study (permesso di soggiorno) at the police headquarters (questura) of the city where you live; the application can be sent through post offices.

You must also complete your actual enrolment. The procedures state that bachelor's and master's applicants who have not enrolled will have their entry visas revoked with effect from **31 January** of the academic year. Renewals of the residence permit must be requested at least 60 days before it expires.

## What to do now

**If you are aiming for 2026-27 (deadline 30 November 2026):**

- Confirm that your Universitaly pre-enrolment has been validated by your university.
- Check your university's own visa deadline — it may already have passed.
- Book the earliest available appointment with the visa service for your city and prepare every document before the appointment.

**If you are aiming for 2027-28 (deadline 31 October 2027):**

- Shortlist programmes now and note each university's admission and visa deadlines. Our [Italy universities directory](/universities/italy/) is a good starting point.
- Follow our [Universitaly pre-enrolment guide](/guides/universitaly-pre-enrolment-guide/) when the 2027-28 window opens on the portal.
- Plan your bank history: keep the funds in your or your parents' account for months, not days.
- Book an English or Italian test early so your B2 certificate is ready before admission.
- Look at [regional grants (DSU) for international students](/scholarships/italy-regional-grants/), which can reduce living costs — but remember an application alone does not count as visa funds.

## Our take for students from Pakistan and South Asia

For many applicants the real risk is timing, not eligibility. In Pakistan, visa appointments, HEC and IBCC attestation, and translations all take weeks, and they stack up in late summer. A student who receives an admission letter in July but starts collecting documents in September can easily miss a university's earlier visa date, even though 31 October 2027 looks far away.

Treat your university's own deadline as the real one, and work backwards from it. Get your degree and transcripts attested early, request your Declaration of Value or CIMEA statement if your university asks for one, and keep your bank statements clean and consistent with your family's income. Read the [Study in Italy](https://studyinitaly.esteri.it/) pages of the Italian Foreign Ministry and the instructions of the visa centre that serves your city, because local document lists can differ.

None of these steps decides the outcome: the final visa decision belongs only to the Italian embassy or consulate, and admission depends on the university. Preparing early simply removes avoidable reasons for delay. For more on Italy, see our [Italy country guide](/countries/italy/).
