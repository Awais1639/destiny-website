The **Erasmus Mundus scholarship 2027** round is already running. There is no single deadline: each programme sets its own, and the European Commission says most of them take applications **between October and January** for courses that start the following academic year. Two programmes we checked today have confirmed scholarship deadlines of **15 December 2026** (NOHA) and **10 January 2027** (Mundus Journalism). If you are aiming for a funded master's in Europe next September, the next seven weeks matter most.

## How the Erasmus Mundus scholarship works

Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters are master's programmes run by a group (a "consortium") of at least three universities in at least three different countries. You usually study in two or more of those countries and finish with a joint degree or more than one degree.

According to the official Erasmus+ website:

- **Who can apply:** students from all over the world. You need a bachelor's degree, or must be in your final year and graduate before the master's starts.
- **Length:** one to two academic years (60, 90 or 120 ECTS credits).
- **Where to apply:** directly to the institution running the programme — not to the European Commission.
- **Scholarships:** programmes may offer full scholarships to the best-ranked students worldwide. These cover participation costs and contribute to travel, visa and living costs.

The EU pages do not give one scholarship amount, because the details are published by each programme. Not every programme in the catalogue offers Erasmus Mundus scholarships, either. EACEA states plainly that "many programs offer Erasmus Mundus scholarships, while some do not."

> The scholarship deadline is often months earlier than the general admission deadline. NOHA's scholarship deadline is 15 December 2026, but its self-funded deadline is not until April. If you need funding, the earlier date is the only one that counts for you.

Remember the time zones too. 23:59 GMT on 15 December is 4:59 a.m. on 16 December in Pakistan, and 13:00 CET on 10 January is 5:00 p.m. in Pakistan. Do not leave the upload to the last hour — Mundus Journalism says its IT support stops on 8 January at 12:00 CET.

## Two programmes closing soon, in more detail

**NOHA (International Humanitarian Action).** The application for the 2027–2029 cohort opened on 1 October 2026 through the programme's portal at [apply.nohanet.org](https://apply.nohanet.org/). The programme's page lists 16 Erasmus Mundus scholarships for this intake, with a grant of EUR 1,400 per month for 24 months, and notes that no more than 10% of scholarships can go to students of the same nationality. For English, NOHA accepts IELTS (6.5 overall, no band under 6.0, not the "home" version), TOEFL iBT or Cambridge C1/C2, with exemptions for applicants whose whole bachelor's was taught in English. See our [NOHA listing](/scholarships/erasmus-mundus/noha/) for the full summary.

**Mundus Journalism (Journalism, Media and Globalisation).** Applications run from 1 November 2026 to 10 January 2027, and the course starts in Aarhus, Denmark, on 1 September. There is no separate scholarship form: admission and Erasmus Mundus scholarship consideration use the same application. You first create an Aarhus University account, then receive a link to the online registration form. English must be at CEFR C1 level or above unless your chosen specialisation exempts you. Our [Mundus Journalism listing](/scholarships/erasmus-mundus/emma/) links to the official pages.

## How to find the other open programmes

These are only two of many programmes. The [Erasmus Mundus Catalogue](https://www.eacea.ec.europa.eu/scholarships/erasmus-mundus-catalogue_en), run by the EU's education agency EACEA, lists the current programmes with links to their websites. You can also browse our own [Erasmus Mundus catalogue](/scholarships/erasmus-mundus/), sorted by subject.

For each programme on your shortlist, check three things on its own website:

1. **Does it offer Erasmus Mundus scholarships this year?** Some do not, or only offer partial or self-funded places.
2. **What is the scholarship deadline?** Write it down with the time zone.
3. **What are the entry requirements?** Subject background, minimum grades, English test and any GRE or portfolio.

Shortlisting three to five programmes in your field is realistic. Applying to many more usually means weaker motivation letters.

## What to do now

- **Pick your programmes this week** and note every scholarship deadline in Pakistan time.
- **Book an English test now** if you need one. IELTS and TOEFL dates in December fill up quickly, and results take time.
- **Request your transcripts and degree** (or a provisional certificate) from your university. If attestation by HEC is needed for later steps, start early.
- **Ask two referees** for recommendation letters before the winter break; see [how to request recommendation letters](/guides/how-to-request-recommendation-letters/).
- **Write a programme-specific motivation letter** for each application. Our guide on [how to write a motivation letter](/guides/how-to-write-a-motivation-letter/) shows a clear structure.
- **Update your CV** in a clear European format — see [how to prepare a scholarship CV](/guides/how-to-prepare-a-scholarship-cv/).
- **Check your grades** against European scales with our [grade converter](/tools/grade-converter/).

## Our take

Erasmus Mundus is one of the most generous master's scholarships open to Pakistani students, and it is very competitive. The students who do well usually apply early to a small number of programmes that genuinely match their bachelor's subject, and explain clearly why that particular combination of universities suits their plans.

A few practical points for applicants from Pakistan and South Asia. Apply only through the programme's official website — there is no HEC nomination and no fee to any agent for "Erasmus selection". Watch the nationality cap: when a programme limits how many scholarships one country can receive, a strong profile matters more than ever. And if you are still in your final year, check whether the programme accepts a provisional transcript now and your final degree later.

Nothing here can promise a place or a scholarship — selection is made by each consortium. But a complete, on-time application to a well-matched programme gives you a fair chance.
